THE SCHOOL
One of the specialized institutions of the Agency for Specialized
Educational Institutions in Khorezm — a national network under the
Ministry of Preschool and School Education that runs Presidential,
Creativity, Sports and specialized schools. Entry is by competitive
examination and every place is fully funded by the state.
The school opened in 2022 under Presidential Decree PF-269 and
specializes in the exact and natural sciences. Instruction is in Uzbek.
Enrolment is 458 across 20 classes — 253 male, 205 female.
Students come mostly from across the Khorezm region. The 576-place
campus holds a three-storey academic building with a dedicated mathematics
block, physics, science and informatics rooms, a library and information
resource centre, a gymnasium, a football pitch and a canteen serving lunch
in two sittings. A purpose-built dormitory completes in autumn 2027.
Entry is by examination at grade 5, in mathematics and English, and at
grade 7, in biology and chemistry; applications run through ariza.piima.uz.
The academic building on Sheroziy Street, Urgench.

Assessment and University Admission
1TWO EXAMINATIONS PER
TERM
The academic year runs in
four terms. In every subject
each term a student sits one
CHSB term examination,
weighted 40–50% of the term
mark, and normally two BSB
standards-based assessments
at 20–25% each. Classwork
supplies the remaining 10%.
Accumulated points convert
to the term mark as 86–
100 = 5, 71–85 = 4, 56–
70 = 3 and below 56 = 2. The
ﬁnal grade in a subject is the
mean of its four term marks.
2THE FIVE-POINT
SCALE
5Excellent A
4Good B
3Satisfactory C
2Unsatisfactory D
0Not completed F
A mark is recorded for
every subject in every
term; the four are
averaged for the year.
3THE DTM EXAMINATION
Admission to national
universities is decided by
the state DTM examination,
marked out of 189 points.
The subject combination
depends on the student’s
intended major, alongside
the mandatory subjects.
Each university sets its own
qualifying score for each
major. In 2026 the lowest
anywhere in the country was
56.7; selective programmes
required far more. A place
is awarded either as a
fully funded state grant or
on a fee-paying contract.
4NATIONAL SUBJECT
CERTIFICATES
Separately from school
marks, the state examines
and certiﬁes attainment
in individual subjects. A
certiﬁcate earned during
school exempts its holder
from that subject’s paper
in the university entrance
examination and credits
full marks for it. A student
holding certiﬁcates in every
subject their intended degree
requires is admitted without
sitting the examination at all.

CURRICULUM
GRADES 5–7
Mathematics, English, Russian, Uzbek language,
literature, integrated science, informatics,
history, robotics, art, physical education and
ethics. Mathematics, English and Russian
carry additional timetabled support lessons.
GRADES 8–11
Integrated science divides into physics,
chemistry, biology and geography, and
students enter one of two specialisms: the
exact sciences, through mathematics and
physics, or the natural sciences, through
biology and chemistry. The specialist syllabus
departs from the standard national curriculum:
subjects within a student’s stream are carried
well beyond general secondary requirements,
to a depth comparable with introductory
university courses in the same discipline.
LANGUAGE AND EXAMINATION
All subjects are taught and examined in Uzbek.
English is a specialist subject from grade 5,
taught on ofﬁcial Cambridge coursebooks
with additional timetabled lessons. Students
holding the national Language Certiﬁcate in
English are exempted from further English
lessons and use the time for their specialism.
